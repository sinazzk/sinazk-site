CASE STUDY 01 · 2024 · LEARNING & AI

SuperReach

Designing an AI-coached learning platform that turns new recruiters into confident callers, and lets managers scale their training.

ROLE
Design lead, Learning Management System · Product designer, Outreach platform
CLIENT
SuperReach: learning & automation for sales and recruitment
TIMELINE
1 year, 2024
TEAM
2 designers, 1 PM, 1 PO, 5 engineers
AT A GLANCE

Problem. Recruiters learned on the job from scattered handbooks and occasional roleplays with busy managers. It was slow to ramp and hard to measure.

What I did. Led research and design of the LMS: a three-level content structure, AI voice roleplays with instant coaching, and a WYSIWYG editor so teams build their own sessions.

Outcome. A polished product with pleasant interactions, and a product-market fit grounded in months of research.

1 month of field study inside a recruitment office
3 levels in the course structure teams build from
7 recruitment companies interviewed
DETAIL 01 · RESEARCH·SHEET 01/05

A month inside a recruitment office

To understand how recruiters actually learn, I volunteered for a month at a recruitment company: observing, asking questions, taking photos and writing reports. That grounded our “Student” persona in their real environment.

For the “Coach” persona, usually the decision-makers, we surveyed then interviewed CEOs, managers and team leads at 7 recruitment companies, and synthesised everything in Dovetail.

FIELD STUDY
1 month on site
INTERVIEWS
CEOs, managers & team leads · 7 companies
SYNTHESIS
Dovetail
PERSONA · STUDENT
New recruiter learning cold calling and the company’s tools. Wants practice without the pressure.
PERSONA · COACH
Team lead or manager. Wants reps ramped faster without spending hours on roleplays.
KEY INSIGHT
Managers didn’t have time to train recruiters and often outsourced the learning, so we took that over.
DETAIL 02 · PROBLEMS & SOLUTIONS·SHEET 02/05

Four problems, one learning system

Videos, PDFs, assignments and assessments had to fit a clear three-level hierarchy (sessions, courses, programs) that new users understood straight away. We tested several structures with users.

To keep learners coming back, the visual design was rebuilt with progress bars, “continue where you left off” and recommendations, and the thumbnail components went through many iterations to show a lot without feeling busy.

HIERARCHY
Sessions → courses → programs
ENGAGEMENT
Progress · resume · recommendations
DETAIL 03 · AI FEATURES·SHEET 03/05

Practise a cold call with an AI prospect, get coached in seconds

After each lesson, reps roleplay a cold call with an AI voice bot. The call screen gives them everything they’d have in a real call, with an animated waveform to make it feel alive.

Afterwards the AI scores the call and points to specific weaknesses and how to improve. Custom GPT personas in a chat interface help with prompts, emails and contact lists.

ROLEPLAY
AI voice prospect · scenario · objective
FEEDBACK
Scored breakdown with suggestions
CHAT
Custom GPTs trained on specialist data
DETAIL 04 · SCALING & DESIGN SYSTEM·SHEET 04/05

Edit a session the way learners will see it

To let a broader market build its own training, the session editor mirrors the learner view, a WYSIWYG approach. Underneath, design decisions became tokens in a Figma and Storybook design system that sped up design and development.

EDITOR
WYSIWYG, mirroring the session page
SYSTEM
Figma + Storybook, token-based
DETAIL 05 · OUTCOME & REFLECTION·SHEET 05/05

What I handed over, and what I learned

I left SuperReach before launch, so I can’t share live numbers. What I handed over was the shared design system and the full UI of the LMS, built on months of research with recruiters and their managers.

The lesson I carried into later AI work: conversation design needs structure and guardrails, especially when someone is learning.

HANDED OVER
Shared design system and the full LMS UI, ready for engineering
LEARNED
Conversation design needs structure and guardrails, especially in a learning experience
