CASE STUDY 02 · 2023 · SERVICE DESIGN & COLLABORATION

Office Culture DJ

Bringing back the hallway chat: a three-month study of how a remote design studio collaborates, and a virtual presence space to fix what it lost.

ROLE
UX researcher & designer for my MSc User Experience Engineering final project
CLIENT
Reason (London) with Goldsmiths, University of London
TIMELINE
3 months, 2023
TEAM
Mostly solo, supervised at Goldsmiths, with ideas from designers at Reason
AT A GLANCE

Problem. Reason went remote after COVID. Tools were adopted ad hoc, relationships siloed, and everyday coordination that took minutes now took hours.

What I did. Studied three teams for three months, mapped their tools and connections, and designed a Slack-native presence space for spontaneous conversation.

Outcome. A prototype test with 8 employees showed a positive effect on collaboration and socialising.

75% reported more efficient communication
66% increase in voluntary adoption
8 employees in the prototype test
DETAIL 01 · CONTEXT·SHEET 01/06

A friendly studio with an empty office

Reason is a London design company with a culture built on trust and fun, yet its Shoreditch office is usually empty. Like many teams after COVID, it adopted remote tools without drawing on research in HCI and computer-supported cooperative work.

LOCATION
Shoreditch, London
SETUP
Hybrid, mostly remote
DURATION
3 months of study
DETAIL 02 · RESEARCH·SHEET 02/06

Mapping how three teams actually work together

A survey and diary study captured routines. Applying the 3C collaboration model (coordination, communication, cooperation) mapped every tool in use, and in-depth interviews surfaced the pain points.

METHODS
Survey · diary study · in-depth interviews
MODEL
3C: coordination, communication, cooperation
TEAMS
Design · engineering · operations
DETAIL 03 · PAIN POINTS·SHEET 03/06

Minutes in the office, hours online

An impact/effort matrix pointed to co-worker communication as the place to intervene.

Booking a meeting, something done constantly at Reason, takes minutes in a room but hours remotely. And online meetings, one of the few chances to socialise, left people dissatisfied.

TOOLS
Impact/effort matrix · service blueprint · journey map
FOCUS
Co-worker communication
DETAIL 04 · CONNECTIONS·SHEET 04/06

Slack and Meet had quietly siloed the company

Network diagrams showed people talking to the same few colleagues and some never interacting with others. The operations team connected with everyone, but top-down social events rarely got people engaging. In the office, small talk happens bottom-up.

Hypothesis: a virtual space for ad-hoc conversation can improve co-workers’ relationships and responsiveness.
DETAIL 05 · DESIGN·SHEET 05/06

A presence space inside Slack

Slack is the communication heart of Reason, so the solution lives there rather than in another tool. Its channel model already supports voice channels, which can act as rooms where you see who is around and drop in.

PLATFORM
Slack, with voice channels as rooms
SIGNALS
Proximity · speaker · mood · mute
DETAIL 06 · TESTING & RESULTS·SHEET 06/06

Engineers used it to work, designers to hang out

8 employees used Tandem and Discord, two tools built around spontaneous conversation, then reflected on whether it improved relationships and efficiency. The effect was positive overall.

The catch: it relied on people remembering to be online. Further work is needed to make presence effortless so conversations start more often.

TESTED WITH
8 Reason employees
TOOLS
Tandem · Discord
FINDING 01 · ENGINEERS
Used it to work. Seeing who was available made quick questions effortless.
FINDING 02 · DESIGNERS
Used it to hang out, with casual chats in free moments.
OPEN QUESTION
It only works if people remember to be present. How do you make showing up effortless?
